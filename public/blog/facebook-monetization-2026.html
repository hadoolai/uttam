1. What Changed in 2026: The Unified "Content Monetization" Engine

Prior to 2026, a creator on Facebook had to maintain eligibility for three distinct programs: In-Stream Ads for horizontal videos, Ads on Facebook Reels for vertical short-form, and the invitation-only Performance Bonus for photo and text reach.

Under the unified Facebook Content Monetization initiative, creators apply once. Upon approval, every piece of eligible public content you publish automatically earns based on its overall performance footprint.

2. The Core Metric: How "Qualified Views" Determine Your Earnings

Meta does not pay purely on raw view counters. Instead, payouts are governed by Qualified Views and Effective RPM (Revenue Per Mille).

A view is classified as Qualified when:

  • The viewer watches past the platform's minimum engagement threshold (typically 3 to 5 seconds for Reels, and 15 to 30 seconds for long-form content).
  • The impression originates from organic distribution (News Feed recommendations, Watch tab, or direct follower feeds) rather than paid click farms or engagement pods.
  • The viewer is located in an ad-monetized geographic region.
  • The content does not trigger copyright disputes or community standard warnings.

Realistic Payout Rates by Audience Tier:

  • Tier 1 Audiences (USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany): $1.50 – $4.50+ RPM
  • Tier 2 Audiences (Middle East, Southern Europe, Latin America): $0.60 – $1.50 RPM
  • Tier 3 Audiences (South Asia, Southeast Asia, East Africa): $0.15 – $0.60 RPM

3. Original vs. Reused Content: Navigating Meta's 2026 Crackdown

Meta's 2026 algorithm update placed an aggressive emphasis on Original Creator Attribution. If your page publishes clips taken from movies, podcast excerpts without a license, or ripped TikToks, your monetization will quickly be blocked by the dreaded "Limited Originality of Content" (LOC) violation.

The Red Flags That Trigger Demonetization:

  1. Third-Party Watermarks: Any video with a visible TikTok, CapCut, InShot, or Xiaohongshu watermark is automatically suppressed from recommendation feeds.
  2. Silent Background Clips: Footage of mobile gameplay (Subway Surfers, GTA car stunts) placed beneath uncredited podcast audio is flagged as "Low-Quality Reused Media".
  3. Automated Scraping Channels: Uploading 10+ identical clips per day scraped from other platforms triggers Meta's spam detection systems.
  4. Music Rights Infringement: Using commercial audio outside of Meta's official Sound Collection results in immediate ad-revenue redirection.

4. AI-Generated Content on Facebook in 2026: Rules & Safe Workflows

Meta allows AI-assisted workflows—such as scripts developed with ChatGPT or Claude, voiceovers from ElevenLabs, and video edits in Descript—provided the creator applies the required "Made with AI" label and adds significant human value, original storytelling, or commentary.

Low-effort synthetic slideshows, generic AI scrapers, or uncurated bulk videos will be penalized for "Limited Originality of Content".

5. Official Eligibility Requirements Checklist

  • Account Status: Professional Mode profile or verified Facebook Page active for at least 30 days.
  • Followers: 5,000 public followers (standard Content Monetization) or 10,000 followers (full-scale In-Stream mid-rolls).
  • View Minutes: At least 60,000 eligible minutes viewed across public videos in the past 60 days.
  • Active Library: At least 5 active, original public videos published on the page.

7. Common Mistakes That Destroy Facebook Earnings

  • Buying Followers or Views: Purchasing bot packages leads to immediate account freezes and failure of the "Qualified Views" metric.
  • Engagement Baiting: Captions like "Type YES to win" or "Share with 5 friends" trigger distribution penalties.
  • Deleting Flagged Videos Prematurely: Always check if an audio segment can be muted or appealed via the Professional Dashboard first.
  • Ignoring Audience Geography: Tailor your content to universal topics that draw higher-tier advertisers to maximize RPM.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the unified Facebook Content Monetization program in 2026?

In 2026, Meta streamlined its previously fragmented monetization features—including In-Stream Ads, Reels Overlay Ads, and the Performance Bonus program—into a single, unified framework called 'Facebook Content Monetization'. Instead of applying for multiple separate programs, eligible creators can monetize Reels, longer videos, photos, and high-engagement text posts through one unified dashboard.

How are "Qualified Views" defined and calculated on Facebook?

Qualified views represent genuine, organic views by real users that meet Meta's engagement and retention thresholds. Casual 1-second scrollers, bot traffic, automated loops, and views on unoriginal or copyright-flagged clips are excluded. Payouts reflect viewer watch time, audience geography, advertiser demand, and content retention.

Can I monetize AI-generated videos and voiceovers on Facebook in 2026?

Yes, but with strict compliance safeguards. Meta allows AI-assisted workflows—such as scripts developed with ChatGPT, voiceovers from ElevenLabs, and video edits in Descript—provided the creator applies the required 'Made with AI' label and adds significant human value, original storytelling, or commentary.

What are the basic eligibility criteria for Facebook monetization in 2026?

Creators need a Page or Professional Mode profile in an eligible country, at least 5,000 to 10,000 followers depending on specific product tiers, and a minimum of 60,000 total eligible minutes viewed on public videos in the last 60 days, along with at least 5 active public videos.
